What the judges listen for

Delivery · how it feels in the room

Feeling that carries
Professors judge the Premier Stage live, not a computer. A speech read one sentence at a time sounds like a list. Keep one feeling across a whole part, then change it at the next heading.
Contrast between parts
The guide is quickest in Project and Message, slowest in Turning point and Closing. The judges hear that change as emotion. If every part sounds the same, the speech sounds flat.
Pauses
/ is a short breath, // a real stop. The long stops after “devotion” and “someone like me” always feel too long to you and just right to the audience.
Eyes and hands
Look up at the questions, show your hands on “tiny ridges”, point to yourself on “someone like me”. Practise them now, so they are natural on the day.

Voice · what this page measures

Melody match
Your rise and fall laid over the guide’s, sentence by sentence. Copy the shape, not the exact notes: a higher or lower voice is never marked down, only a flatter one.
Voice range
How far your voice moves, from its low notes to its high notes, next to the guide’s. This is the number for feeling: a reading that moves less than the guide sounds calm and flat, which costs the most marks in empathy. Aim to match it or go past it.
Stress
Bold, underlined words carry the stress. Make them louder, longer and higher than the words around them. Shaded columns under the curve are the same words.

How to practise

  1. Listen once all the way through with your eyes on the script. Do not speak yet.
  2. Switch to Repeat after me and allow the microphone. The guide stops after every part and records you while you say the whole part back, then lays your melody over its own and shows your voice range.
  3. A part with a low match or a small range: press Play the guide, listen for where the voice rises and slows, and record it again. To drill one part on its own, press Record this part.
  4. When the parts feel easy, press Record it in one go. Read straight through and finish between 1:45 and 1:58.
  5. Learning it by heart: in the one-go screen, press Hide the words. Only the first words of each part stay, so you practise remembering it as you will on stage.
  6. Send your best one-go recording to your teacher from Your speech.
